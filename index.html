More than a match

A simple idea, shared around the world.

Ball games have been played in many cultures for centuries, but modern association football has a more specific starting point. In 1863, the newly formed Football Association in England established a common set of rules. From there, schools, clubs, railways, migration, and international competition helped carry the game across continents. Its local roots remain part of its appeal: almost anyone can find a place to play.

The game through time

Milestones that moved football forward

Each era brought new players, new audiences, and a wider stage.

1863

A shared rulebook

The Football Association is formed in England and publishes a common code of rules.

1904

A global federation

FIFA is founded in Paris to support and organize international football.

1930

A world stage

Uruguay hosts the first men's FIFA World Cup, bringing 13 national teams together.

1991 onward

The women's game grows

The first FIFA Women's World Cup is held in China. In 2023, the tournament expands to 32 teams.

A game still growing

More teams. More voices. More ways to play.

Football's growth is bigger than any one tournament. Community clubs and youth programs open doors for the next generation; women's competitions reach new milestones; and broadcasts connect supporters across time zones. The 2026 men's World Cup expands to 48 teams, while the 2023 Women's World Cup featured 32. The game keeps evolving—and its future belongs to everyone who plays, watches, coaches, and supports it.

The next chapter is still being written.

From a neighborhood kickabout to a packed stadium, football continues to connect people through teamwork, identity, and the thrill of the game. Wherever the next generation takes it, that shared spirit stays at the heart of football's story.
